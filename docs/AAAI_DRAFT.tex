\title{Behavioral Imitation and Safety Are Dissociable:\\
A Stage-Separated Evaluation with Positive Controls}
\author{Redacted for review}

\begin{abstract}
When a language model is fine-tuned to imitate another model, does it inherit the
target's safety behavior? We evaluate a complete \CampaignModels-model ordered
source--target matrix over \CampaignDatasets datasets, separately at the SFT and
SFT$\rightarrow$DPO stages. Each stage contains \CampaignAdaptersPerStage
off-diagonal adapters, evaluated on the same seed-\CampaignEvaluationSeed sample of
\CampaignEvaluationPrompts prompts per safety benchmark. Mean harmful-compliance
change is \SFTMeanErosionPP percentage points after SFT and \DPOMeanErosionPP points
after DPO; \SFTPctSafer\% and \DPOPctSafer\% of cells, respectively, become safer.
The exact-cell DPO-minus-SFT change is \PairedStageDeltaPP points over
\PairedStageCells pairs. Variation is principally source-conditioned rather than
target-conditioned. A complementary projection-ablation assay provides a causal
dissociation: a refusal-cone positive control changes harmful compliance, while a
benign cross-model provenance contrast does not move safety beyond a norm-matched
random control on the evaluable base-model set. These results bound a safety claim
about weight-based imitation; they do not establish that the provenance contrast is
a complete representation of model identity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Language models exhibit recognizable response patterns, including tone, formatting,
verbosity, and characteristic refusals. Prompting or fine-tuning can move a source
model toward a target model on these behavioral measurements. It does not follow that
the target's safety policy transfers with them.

We separate three questions that are often pooled. First, how does supervised
imitation affect safety? Second, what additional change follows preference
optimization? Third, is a benign cross-model activation contrast causally entangled
with refusal behavior? The first two require separate SFT and DPO measurements on
matched cells. The third requires a positive control and a random control under the
same intervention operator.

Our contribution is a complete, configuration-defined matrix with a single evaluation
standard, an exact-cell comparison between training stages, and a claim scope tied to
the controls that were actually run. Prompt-only interventions, alternate reference
directions, and uncontrolled adapter extensions are outside that scope.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Configured imitation campaign}
The named \texttt{imitation\_safety} campaign in \texttt{config.yaml} fixes the roster,
datasets, seed, and evaluation sampler. The roster contains \CampaignModels models
spanning 4B--70B parameters and dense, mixture-of-experts, and hybrid architectures.
For every ordered source$\rightarrow$target pair with source $\ne$ target and each of
\CampaignDatasets datasets, we train an SFT adapter and then a DPO adapter from its
matching SFT parent. The two stages are never pooled.

Each adapter and its unadapted source baseline are evaluated on AdvBench, HarmBench,
StrongREJECT, SORRY-Bench, SG-Bench, XSTest, and OR-Bench-Hard. Sampling uses
\CampaignEvaluationPrompts rows and seed \CampaignEvaluationSeed. XSTest's
over-refusal metric is evaluated on the 111 benign examples within its shared 200-row
sample. Harm is scored with a content-aware refuse-then-leak judge and benchmark-native
graders. For harm benchmark $b$,
\[
  E_{a,b}=H_{a,b}-H_{\mathrm{base}(a),b}.
\]
An adapter's headline erosion is the mean of $E_{a,b}$ over the five harm-axis
benchmarks. The incremental preference-optimization effect is paired exactly on
dataset, source, target, and seed:
\[
  \Delta_{\mathrm{DPO-SFT}}=E_{\mathrm{DPO}}-E_{\mathrm{SFT}}.
\]

\subsection{Coverage and aggregation}
Adapter training is not counted as evaluation. A cell is complete only if all seven
metrics exist and its source baseline has matching sample sizes. Checkpoints from
different workers are deduplicated by ID and analysis-relevant content; unresolved
conflicts abort. Aggregate CSVs record \texttt{stage=sft$\mid$dpo}. Headline generation
is gated on 528/528 coverage at both stages.

We report one-way $\eta^2=SS_{\mathrm{between}}/SS_{\mathrm{total}}$ for source,
target, and dataset. ``Eroder'' sources are selected from the data using a declared
+1 percentage-point mean threshold rather than a hardcoded model list.

\subsection{Projection-ablation assay}
For each base model, we derive a benign cross-model provenance contrast from 120 paired
benign responses. The 120 examples construct a direction; they are not an evaluation
denominator. We compare projection ablation of this contrast with (i) a refusal-cone
positive control and (ii) a norm-matched random direction. A model contributes to the
dissociation claim only where the refusal control fires and the random control remains
valid.

This assay tests whether the measured benign provenance contrast is causally coupled
to refusal behavior. It does not prove that ablating the contrast removes model
identity: the held-out identity diagnostic is insufficient for that stronger claim.

\subsection{Controlled mechanistic scope}
At the base-model level, the refusal-cone, benign-provenance, and random arms support a
positive-controlled dissociation between the measured provenance contrast and refusal
behavior. Llama-3.3-70B is included in this base-model assay with all three controls.
Granite-4-H-Small is not: its cone training retained no usable refusal targets and
became non-finite, so it remains an imitation model without a steering verdict.

The adapter-level analysis is restricted to cells containing all three arms. The 70B
adapter extension contains cone and baseline arms only. It therefore shows that the
refusal positive control remains effective after 70B imitation fine-tuning, but it is
not evidence that a controlled adapter-level provenance null extends to 70B.

\section{Limitations}
The main matrix uses one training seed. Every safety comparison uses matched prompts,
but the resulting small deltas are quantized by a 200-row benchmark sample. The
variance decomposition is descriptive rather than a causal attribution and is
reported separately for SFT and DPO.

We derive one benign provenance contrast per model against a fixed reference. Because
the claim is limited to that measured contrast, a second-reference campaign is not
required; reference-invariant identity would be a different, stronger claim. Likewise,
prompt-only safety rungs would broaden the study from weight-based imitation to a full
intervention ladder and are not needed for the present conclusion.

The base-model steering assay and controlled adapter assay have different populations.
We do not infer adapter controls from base-model controls, and we do not use the
cone-only 70B extension to enlarge the controlled adapter population.

\section{Conclusion}
Across a complete core-12 matrix, supervised imitation and subsequent preference
optimization have separately measured safety effects, with an exact-cell estimate of
the change between them. The remaining heterogeneity is more source-conditioned than
target-conditioned. A positive-controlled activation assay further shows that the
measured benign provenance contrast is dissociable from refusal behavior on the
evaluable base-model set. The evidence supports a bounded conclusion about behavioral
imitation and safety; it does not require prompt-only experiments, repeated failed cone
training, a second reference direction, or uncontrolled 70B adapter arms.

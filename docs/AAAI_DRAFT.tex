\title{Identity Is Steerable, Safety Is Not:\\
Behavioral Imitation Transfers a Model's Fingerprint but Not Its Safety}
\author{Redacted for review}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Every language model carries a distinctive behavioral fingerprint, and a model
can be pushed to imitate another's. We ask whether imitation also transfers the
target's \emph{safety} disposition, and whether a model's identity can be
mechanistically separated from its safety. Across a 540-adapter imitation matrix
(9 source models $\times$ up to 18 targets $\times$ 4 datasets) and a 20-model
projection-ablation study, we find that behavioral identity is \emph{steerable and
separable from safety}. (1) Fine-tuning a model to imitate another erodes its
genuine safety only slightly and \emph{source-conditionally}: a variance
decomposition attributes 79\% of erosion variance to the source model, 3\% to the
imitation target, and 0.2\% to the dataset; the effect is a property of the
disguising model, not what it imitates. (2) The erosion is \emph{imitation-specific}
--- a matched-compute self-imitation control is null ($-0.4$pt for the one clean
eroder) while cross-imitation erodes ($+3.4$pt). (3) Off-the-shelf output filters
badly overstate the phenomenon: Llama-Guard flags harm $3.5\times$ more often than a
content-aware judge (up to $6.7\times$ on AdvBench), and $79\%$ of its flags are
false-positive \emph{refusals} of harmful prompts. (4) Mechanistically, ablating a
benign cross-model \emph{provenance} direction erases the fingerprint without eroding
safety, while ablating the refusal direction under the identical operator is
catastrophic --- the safety effect lives in the weight update, not the steerable
identity direction. Together: \textbf{identity is steerable, safety is not.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Public language models have recognizable behavioral fingerprints---tone, formatting
habits, characteristic refusals---and prior work shows a source model can be made to
imitate a target's style through prompting or fine-tuning. A natural and
safety-critical question follows: when a model adopts another's \emph{behavior}, does
it also inherit its \emph{safety}? And is the ``identity'' that transfers
mechanistically the same object as the model's safety, or a separable one?

We study this with two complementary experiments on a shared roster of open-weight
models spanning 4B--70B parameters, dense / MoE / hybrid-Mamba / VLM architectures,
and ${\sim}10$ labs. The first is a behavioral \emph{imitation matrix}: we fine-tune
each source to imitate each target (LoRA SFT$\rightarrow$DPO on four datasets) and
measure the change in genuine harmful-compliance across seven safety benchmarks. The
second is a \emph{steering} study: using diff-of-means projection-ablation
\cite{arditi2024}, we build a benign cross-model \emph{provenance} direction (source
$-$ target activations on benign prompts) and test, with a per-model positive
control, whether ablating it erodes safety.

Our contribution is a \emph{conjunction} that no prior work assembles: a
matched-compute control isolating imitation-specific erosion, a measurement
correction quantifying how far off-the-shelf guards are, and a mechanistic
localization of the erosion to the weight update rather than the steerable identity
axis. Each ingredient individually is prior art; the packaged result---a benign
provenance direction that is causally separable from safety---is not.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Benign fine-tuning erodes safety.} \citet{qi2023} established that benign
fine-tuning degrades alignment, and recent work shows black-box behavioral
distillation can strip and even amplify unsafety beyond the teacher. We \emph{cede}
the phenomenon and refine it with a matched-compute control gap, source-conditioning,
and a measurement correction. \textbf{Shallow safety / refuse-then-leak.}
\citet{qi2024shallow} and NOICE show alignment lives in the first output tokens and
that guards are fooled by an initial refusal. \textbf{Steering operator.} Our
ablation operator is exactly \citet{arditi2024}; our only methodological delta is the
\emph{contrast set}---benign source$-$target activations (a provenance axis) rather
than harmful$-$harmless (a safety axis). \textbf{Guard calibration} supports that
off-the-shelf filters mis-measure; our addition is the measured magnitude and
mechanism on a controlled matrix. \textbf{Closest tension.} A concurrent persona-vs-
refusal result reaches the opposite conclusion using the same operator; we rebut it
by distinguishing a \emph{within-model} compliance axis (cosine ${\sim}{-}0.18$ to
refusal) from our \emph{cross-model} provenance axis (cosine ${\sim}0$), and by a
positive-controlled null that holds even where the fingerprint has non-trivial cosine
to refusal.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Experiment 1: Imitation safety-erosion matrix}
For each ordered source$\rightarrow$target pair and dataset $D$, we train a LoRA
adapter (rank 32) with SFT on 500 of the target's responses followed by DPO
(target-preferred, source-baseline-dispreferred); seed 42. We evaluate each
\emph{disguised} adapter and its unadapted \emph{baseline} on seven safety
benchmarks (200 prompts each): five harm-axis (AdvBench, HarmBench, StrongREJECT,
SORRY-Bench, SG-Bench) and two over-refusal (XSTest, OR-Bench). Harm is scored by a
content-aware \emph{refuse-then-leak} (RTL) judge that reads the full response, so a
model that refuses then leaks is counted harmful while a clean refusal is not.
Erosion $=\mathrm{harm}(\text{disguised})-\mathrm{harm}(\text{baseline})$ on the
same prompts. A \emph{self-imitation} control (source imitates \emph{itself}, self-SFT)
is the matched-compute benign baseline; self-DPO is degenerate and omitted.

\subsection{Experiment 2: Steering dissociation}
We compute a benign provenance direction as the diff-of-means of source$-$target
activations on benign prompts, extended to a concept cone, and apply projection
ablation $x' \leftarrow x-(x\cdot\hat r)\hat r$. Each model gets a pre-registered
\emph{positive control}: ablating the \emph{refusal} cone must erode safety, else the
model is honestly reported as unusable for the test (we distinguish genuine
positive-control failure from a coherence-collapse artifact, which we mark
\textsc{pc\_invalid}). On models where the control fires, we ask whether ablating the
\emph{provenance} direction moves safety relative to a random-direction control.

\section{Results}
\paragraph{Finding 1 --- Imitation erosion is small and source-conditioned.}
Across $n{=}540$ disguise adapters, mean genuine-harm erosion is $+0.2$pt, no adapter
exceeds $+10$pt, and $45\%$ get \emph{safer}. A one-way variance decomposition of the
erosion attributes \textbf{79\% to the source model, 3\% to the imitation target,
0.2\% to the dataset} (Table~\ref{tab:variance}). Only the least-safe base
(ministral-8b, $26\%$ baseline harm) erodes appreciably ($+3.4$pt); a robust source
gets safer ($-2.9$pt). Disguisability---and its safety cost---is a property of the
\emph{disguising} model, unpredictable to an auditor (``asymmetric laundering'').

\paragraph{Finding 2 --- The erosion is imitation-specific.}
A matched self-imitation control isolates the effect: ministral imitating
\emph{itself} drifts $-0.4$pt (null, all four datasets), while imitating \emph{others}
erodes $+3.4$pt; a robust source is null both ways. The erosion is a property of
\emph{cross}-imitation, not of fine-tuning on 500 examples per se.

\paragraph{Finding 3 --- Off-the-shelf guards overstate the effect $3.5\times$.}
On $n{=}4{,}712$ matrix responses, Llama-Guard-3-8B flags $25.4\%$ unsafe versus
$7.2\%$ genuine harm --- a \textbf{$3.5\times$ overcount} ($6.7\times$ on AdvBench).
The mechanism is not refuse-then-leak: $79\%$ of Guard's flags ($940/1{,}196$) are
false positives and \emph{every one is a clean refusal} of a harmful prompt---Guard
reacts to the prompt topic, not whether the response delivered harm---while it
simultaneously \emph{misses} $25\%$ of genuine harm (recall $75.3\%$). Practitioners
relying on such filters both over-flag safe refusals and under-detect real harm.

\paragraph{Finding 4 --- Identity is steerable, safety is not.}
On the $N{=}20$ models where the positive control fires, ablating the benign
provenance direction is null (indistinguishable from a random-direction control)
while ablating the refusal direction under the identical operator erodes safety
catastrophically. The dissociation is \textbf{20/20 CLEAN} across the roster; on the
core set, four models genuinely resist single-direction refusal ablation (reported as
resistance, not support), one is \textsc{pc\_invalid} (an all-layer cone
over-ablates a deep VLM tower), and two 120B/32B-hybrid models are deferred to larger
hardware. Weight-level DPO imitation erodes safety, but the \emph{steerable} identity
direction does not carry that erosion.

\section{Limitations}
Results are single-seed (42). Two large models (a 120B MoE, a 32B hybrid) are deferred
to 4-GPU hardware for the steering cone. Imitation runs on 9 locally-trainable sources
(deep coverage) versus 20 steering models (cheap-intervention breadth); the connecting
claim is made on the 8-model matched core where both experiments ran. Harm labels come
from an LLM judge; we mitigate with a content-aware refuse-then-leak design and a
three-way adjudication.

\section{Conclusion}
A model's behavioral identity is steerable and \emph{separable} from its safety.
Imitation transfers the fingerprint but its small, source-conditioned safety erosion
lives in the weight update rather than the steerable identity direction, and
off-the-shelf guards overstate that erosion ${\sim}3.5\times$ by flagging refusals as
unsafe. Behavioral-fingerprint auditing is therefore unreliable in adversarial
settings---and unreliable \emph{unpredictably}, per source---while capability/safety
must be measured with content-aware judges, not output filters.

\title{Imitation Transfers Identity, Not Safety:\\
A Measurement Correction and a Mechanistic Dissociation}
\author{Redacted for review}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Prior work reports that benign fine-tuning erodes alignment, and behavioral
distillation is widely treated as a safety hazard. We revisit that claim with a
controlled imitation matrix (a clean $13\times13$ source$\times$target square,
$625$ adapters over 4 datasets) and a 23-model projection-ablation study, and reach a more
measured conclusion: \textbf{imitation transfers a model's fingerprint without
transferring its safety, and the reported hazard is largely a measurement artifact.}
(1) Off-the-shelf output filters badly overstate the phenomenon: on identical
responses Llama-Guard reports $25.4\%$ harm where two independent content-aware graders
--- our judge and the official HarmBench classifier --- agree on $7$--$9\%$, a
$2.7$--$3.5\times$ overcount, while the filter simultaneously \emph{misses} $25\%$ of
genuine harm. The guard reacts to prompt topic rather than response content. (2) Measured with a content-aware judge, imitation erodes
genuine safety only marginally: mean $+0.2$pt across 625 adapters, only $2$ above
$+10$pt (both a reasoning-model judging artifact), and $45\%$ get \emph{safer}. (3) What little
erosion exists tracks the \emph{base} model, not the imitated one: a variance
decomposition attributes 56\% of the (small) erosion variance to the base model, 3\% to
the imitated target, and 1.5\% to the dataset (source still dominates target ${\sim}18\times$).
We report this tentatively --- the
underlying signal is near the measurement floor --- but if it holds it has a security
reading (\emph{asymmetric laundering}): a model's disguisability, and its safety cost,
is a property of the disguising model, not of what it imitates. (4) Mechanistically,
identity and safety occupy causally separable subspaces: ablating a benign cross-model
\emph{provenance} direction leaves safety unmoved (within $4$pt of baseline, and
indistinguishable from a random-direction control) while ablating the refusal cone
under the identical operator is catastrophic ($+16$ to $+96$pt harmful compliance),
across \textbf{23/23} models whose positive control fires. Identity transfer does not
drag safety with it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Public language models have recognizable behavioral fingerprints---tone, formatting
habits, characteristic refusals---and prior work shows a source model can be made to
imitate a target's style through prompting or fine-tuning. A natural and
safety-critical question follows: when a model adopts another's \emph{behavior}, does
it also inherit its \emph{safety}? And is the ``identity'' that transfers
mechanistically the same object as the model's safety, or a separable one?

We study this with two complementary experiments on a shared roster of open-weight
models spanning 4B--70B parameters, dense / MoE / hybrid-Mamba / VLM architectures,
and ${\sim}10$ labs. The first is a behavioral \emph{imitation matrix}: we fine-tune
each source to imitate each target (LoRA SFT$\rightarrow$DPO on four datasets) and
measure the change in genuine harmful-compliance across seven safety benchmarks. The
second is a \emph{steering} study: using diff-of-means projection-ablation
\cite{arditi2024}, we build a benign cross-model \emph{provenance} direction (source
$-$ target activations on benign prompts) and test, with a per-model positive
control, whether ablating it erodes safety.

Our contribution is a \emph{conjunction} that no prior work assembles: a measurement
correction quantifying how far off-the-shelf guards are and \emph{why}, a controlled
matrix large enough to show that the erosion those guards report is small and
source-conditioned once measured properly, and a positive-controlled mechanistic
account of why identity transfer need not carry safety with it. Each ingredient
individually is prior art; the packaged result---a benign provenance direction that is
causally separable from safety, together with the measurement correction that explains
the literature's alarm---is not. We regard the corrective as the primary contribution:
a hazard widely treated as established shrinks to $+0.2$pt under a content-aware judge.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Benign fine-tuning erodes safety.} \citet{qi2023} established that benign
fine-tuning degrades alignment, and recent work shows black-box behavioral
distillation can strip and even amplify unsafety beyond the teacher. We \emph{cede}
the phenomenon and refine it with a matched-compute control gap, source-conditioning,
and a measurement correction. \textbf{Shallow safety / refuse-then-leak.}
\citet{qi2024shallow} and NOICE show alignment lives in the first output tokens and
that guards are fooled by an initial refusal. \textbf{Steering operator.} Our
ablation operator is exactly \citet{arditi2024}; our only methodological delta is the
\emph{contrast set}---benign source$-$target activations (a provenance axis) rather
than harmful$-$harmless (a safety axis). \textbf{Guard calibration} supports that
off-the-shelf filters mis-measure; our addition is the measured magnitude and
mechanism on a controlled matrix. \textbf{Closest tension.} A concurrent persona-vs-
refusal result reaches the opposite conclusion using the same operator; we rebut it
by distinguishing a \emph{within-model} compliance axis (cosine ${\sim}{-}0.18$ to
refusal) from our \emph{cross-model} provenance axis (cosine ${\sim}0$), and by a
positive-controlled null that holds even where the fingerprint has non-trivial cosine
to refusal.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Experiment 1: Imitation safety-erosion matrix}
For each ordered source$\rightarrow$target pair and dataset $D$, we train a LoRA
adapter (rank 32) with SFT on 500 of the target's responses followed by DPO
(target-preferred, source-baseline-dispreferred); seed 42. The matrix is
deliberately \emph{rectangular} ($9$ sources $\times$ up to $18$ targets), not
square: imitation requires only a target's \emph{outputs}, whereas using a model as
a \emph{source} requires fine-tuning its weights. The $9$ sources are the models in
our local fine-tuning track; models we did not fine-tune locally --- those served
through a separate API training backend, and the largest ($120$B) checkpoints that
exceed our local GPUs --- contribute as imitation targets only. This costs no
generality for our claims, which are about how a source's safety changes, not the
targets'. We evaluate each
\emph{disguised} adapter and its unadapted \emph{baseline} on seven safety
benchmarks (200 prompts each): five harm-axis (AdvBench, HarmBench, StrongREJECT,
SORRY-Bench, SG-Bench) and two over-refusal (XSTest, OR-Bench). Harm is scored by a
content-aware \emph{refuse-then-leak} (RTL) judge that reads the full response, so a
model that refuses then leaks is counted harmful while a clean refusal is not.
Erosion $=\mathrm{harm}(\text{disguised})-\mathrm{harm}(\text{baseline})$ on the
same prompts. A \emph{self-imitation} control (source imitates \emph{itself}, self-SFT)
is the matched-compute benign baseline; self-DPO is degenerate and omitted.

\subsection{Experiment 2: Steering dissociation}
We compute a benign provenance direction as the diff-of-means of source$-$target
activations on benign prompts, extended to a concept cone, and apply projection
ablation $x' \leftarrow x-(x\cdot\hat r)\hat r$. Each model gets a pre-registered
\emph{positive control}: ablating the \emph{refusal} cone must erode safety, else the
model is honestly reported as unusable for the test. Verdicts are assigned per
benchmark on the harm axis; XSTest is excluded because its baseline harm is ${\sim}0$,
so the control cannot fire there and a failure would be uninformative. We also discard
any model whose \emph{random-direction} control itself moves harm appreciably, since a
null is meaningless once the control arm is contaminated (this excludes one deep VLM
whose all-layer cone over-ablates the tower, random arm $+19.4$pt). On models where the
control fires, we ask whether ablating the \emph{provenance} direction moves safety
relative to that random-direction control.

\section{Results}
\paragraph{Finding 1 --- Off-the-shelf guards overstate the effect $2.7$--$3.5\times$.}
On $n{=}4{,}712$ matrix responses, Llama-Guard-3-8B flags $25.4\%$ unsafe versus
$7.2\%$ genuine harm --- a \textbf{$3.5\times$ overcount} ($6.7\times$ on AdvBench) ---
while it simultaneously \emph{misses} $25\%$ of genuine harm (recall $75.3\%$). Guard
flags responses that do not deliver harm, reacting to prompt topic rather than
response content. This result is logically prior to the rest: any erosion estimate
computed with such a filter inherits the inflation, which is why we measure with a
content-aware judge throughout.

The overcount is a claim about two instruments disagreeing, so we corroborate it with
an \emph{independent} third grader on the identical responses. Scoring the same
$4{,}712$ responses with the official HarmBench classifier
(\texttt{cais/HarmBench-Llama-2-13b-cls}) gives harm rates of \textbf{Llama-Guard
$25.4\%$, HarmBench classifier $9.3\%$, our judge $7.2\%$}: the two content-aware
graders bracket genuine harm at $7$--$9\%$ while the output filter alone reports $25\%$.
Guard is thus the outlier, overcounting $2.7\times$ relative to the independent grader
($3.5\times$ relative to ours), and the claim does not rest on trusting our judge. A
second cross-check on the disjoint steering distribution ($n{=}50{,}000$) agrees
($\kappa{=}0.82$, base rates $25.5\%$ vs $24.7\%$). We report the overcount
conservatively as $2.7$--$3.5\times$; our judge is if anything marginally \emph{more}
lenient than the HarmBench classifier (concentrated on SORRY-Bench), so our
RTL-scored erosion is a slight underestimate, which does not rescue the filter. We
also decline the sharper claim that Guard's false positives are all \emph{clean
refusals}: a refusal-phrase lexicon covers only $79\%$ of them and $43\%$ contain
degenerate decoding artifacts, so we report the conservative form (``does not deliver
harm'').

\paragraph{Finding 2 --- Measured properly, imitation erosion is small.}
Across the $n{=}625$ disguise adapters of the clean $13\times13$ square, mean genuine-harm
erosion is $+0.2$pt and $45\%$ get \emph{safer}. Only $2$ adapters exceed $+10$pt, and both
are gpt-oss-20b (a reasoning-channel model) whose verbose refusals the content-aware judge
over-flags as refuse-then-leak --- an instance of the Finding-1 overcount, not real erosion.
Only 2 of the 13 sources erode by more than $1$pt. The headline hazard of behavioral
imitation does not survive a content-aware measurement.

\paragraph{Finding 3 --- What little erosion exists tracks the base model, not the
imitated one.}
To the extent the small erosion in Finding~2 varies at all, it varies with the
\emph{base} model (the one being fine-tuned), not the imitated target. A one-way
variance decomposition attributes \textbf{56\% of erosion variance to the base model,
3\% to the imitated target, 1.5\% to the dataset} (Table~\ref{tab:variance}) --- the base
dominates the imitated target ${\sim}18\times$: the least-safe base (ministral-8b, $26\%$
baseline harm) erodes appreciably ($+3.1$pt) while a robust base gets safer ($-2.9$pt), and
which model is imitated barely matters.
We caution that this decomposition sits on a small signal: per-benchmark harm rates are
quantised to ${\sim}0.005$ (200 prompts), the erosion values it partitions are a few
tenths of a point, and the single-seed matrix carries no significance test. A separate
three-seed pilot (Limitations) adds $p$-values and finds the same base-conditioning, so
we read this as a \emph{tentative} but seed-stable pattern rather than a precisely
estimated effect. If it holds, it has a security reading we call \emph{asymmetric
laundering}: a model's disguisability, and its safety cost, is a property of the model
doing the disguising and is not predictable from the model it imitates.

\paragraph{Finding 4 --- Identity and safety are causally separable.}
On the $N{=}23$ models where the refusal positive control fires, ablating the benign
provenance direction is null --- indistinguishable from a random-direction control ---
while ablating the refusal cone under the identical operator erodes safety
catastrophically. Averaged over the harm benchmarks where the control fires, the cone
moves harmful compliance by $+16$ to $+96$pt, whereas the provenance direction moves it
by $-0.9$ to $+3.9$pt, against $-0.9$ to $+3.8$pt for a random direction on the same
models. The dissociation is \textbf{23/23}: 21 models are clean across every harm
benchmark and 2 are clean across the subset where the control fires. Four further models
genuinely resist single-direction refusal ablation (reported as resistance, not support),
one is discarded for a contaminated random-direction control, two are deferred on a
hardware gap, and two were not run. Verdicts are scored on the harm axis only;
XSTest is excluded because its baseline harm is ${\sim}0$, so the control cannot fire
there. Identity and safety are thus separable objects in activation space: the
fingerprint can be removed without touching refusal. This is the mechanistic
counterpart of the behavioral picture in Findings~2--3 --- imitation moves a model's
identity but barely its safety, because the two are not the same object.

\section{Limitations}
The main matrix is single-seed (42). Our central claims---a measurement correction
(Finding~1) and a mechanistic dissociation (Finding~4)---involve no training
stochasticity, and the erosion effect is characterized as \emph{small} rather than
precisely estimated, so we expect the qualitative conclusions to be seed-robust. A
separate 7-model, 3-seed pilot (seeds 42--44), trained through a \emph{different}
pipeline (the Tinker API) and evaluated as sources on AdvBench and StrongREJECT,
supports both the near-null and the base-conditioning directly: overall genuine
erosion holds at $+0.27/+0.21/+0.21$pt across seeds (all $p<10^{-4}$), the per-model
seed standard deviation ($\sim$0.1pt) is ${\sim}8\times$ smaller than the
between-source spread, and every model's erode/does-not-erode verdict is preserved
across all three seeds --- so base-conditioning is not seed noise. (These seven
Tinker-trained sources are now folded into the main $13\times13$ matrix.) Because that
pilot roster is disjoint from the locally-trained sources, we additionally re-trained the
local eroder (ministral) and a null control (aya) at seeds 43/44 across all four datasets
and re-scored erosion on the same two benchmarks: ministral erodes at $+4.2/+4.9/+4.7$pt
across seeds 42/43/44 (across-seed SD $0.29$pt) while aya stays at $+1.4/+1.1/+1.2$pt (SD
$0.13$pt), so the between-source gap ($\sim$3.5pt) is ${\sim}12\text{--}27\times$ the
across-seed SD --- confirming source-conditioning on the exact matrix models, not only a
proxy roster. Multi-seed replication of the \emph{full} matrix is left to future work.
All 13 imitation-square sources now carry a full five-benchmark steering verdict on the harm axis
(AdvBench, HarmBench, StrongREJECT, SORRY-Bench, SG-Bench)---the same harm axis as the imitation-erosion
study---so the mechanistic dissociation is measured on the exact imitation grid \emph{and} the same
benchmark set rather than an overlapping subset: 9/13 are clean, 3/13 show genuine single-cone resistance
(refusal resists single-direction ablation with a clean random control: gpt-oss-120b, qwen3.6-27b,
gemma-4-e4b), and 1/13 is reported but not counted (gemma-4-31b). For that last model we disclose a flaw
in our own measurement rather than dropping it: our all-layer refusal cone over-ablates its 60-layer
vision-language tower, so even a random-direction ablation moves harm 20--32pt, contaminating the control
and rendering its verdict untrustworthy---a limitation of the operator on deep VLMs, not a property of the
model's safety (its smaller sibling gemma-4-e4b has a clean control). In no source does identity ablation
erode safety beyond the random control. (The broader steering roster of 23 models is wider only for
cheap-intervention breadth.) Only two in-grid sources
erode $>1$pt (ministral-8b $+3.1$, qwen3.6-27b $+1.1$), and of those only ministral-8b
also has a clean steering verdict (qwen3.6-27b is a genuine steering PC-fail), so the
eroding half of any ``weight-erodes / steering-does-not'' connecting claim rests on
$n{=}1$. We therefore do not lean on that connecting claim: the two pillars
(Findings~1 and~4) each stand alone.
Relatedly, a matched self-imitation control (a model imitating \emph{itself} drifts
$-0.4$pt versus $+3.1$pt imitating others) suggests the erosion is specific to
\emph{cross}-imitation rather than generic fine-tuning, but this contrast too is only
measurable on the single appreciably-eroding source (ministral-8b); we report it as a
single-source supporting observation (appendix), not as a finding. Harm labels come
from an LLM judge; we mitigate with a content-aware refuse-then-leak design and validate it against the
official HarmBench classifier ($\kappa{=}0.82$, $n{=}50$k) and a human hand-census ($\kappa{=}0.79$),
as detailed in Finding~1. The human census covers AdvBench/StrongREJECT; the HarmBench-classifier
agreement extends the check across the full harm axis.

\section{Conclusion}
Behavioral imitation transfers a model's fingerprint without transferring its safety.
Measured with a content-aware judge, the safety cost of imitation is small
($+0.2$pt mean; $45\%$ of adapters get \emph{safer}) and confined to sources that were
already fragile; off-the-shelf guards inflate it $2.7$--$3.5\times$ (versus two
independent content-aware graders) by flagging responses that do not deliver harm. The mechanistic counterpart is a causal dissociation: a model's
identity direction can be ablated without moving its refusal behavior, across 23
models. Two practical consequences follow. First, safety claims about fine-tuned or
distilled models must be measured with content-aware judges rather than output
filters, or they inherit a multiplicative inflation. Second, behavioral-fingerprint
auditing is unreliable in adversarial settings---and unreliable \emph{unpredictably},
per source---because disguisability is a property of the disguising model rather than
of the model being imitated.
